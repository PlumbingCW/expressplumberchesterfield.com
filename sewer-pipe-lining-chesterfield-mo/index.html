What Is Sewer Pipe Lining? A Chesterfield, MO Guide

Lining repairs a cracked or root-invaded sewer pipe from the inside, but only some pipe problems qualify.

Sewer pipe lining, also called cured-in-place pipe lining, is a way to repair a damaged sewer line without digging a full trench. A flexible tube soaked in resin is pulled into the old pipe, expanded against the walls and hardened. The result is a new pipe inside the old one. In Chesterfield, MO, homeowners ask about it when roots, cracks or repeated backups point to a failing line.

Lining is not a cure for every problem. Offsets, collapsed sections and low spots often rule it out, and a camera inspection decides whether it fits. This guide explains how lining works, what it can and cannot fix, what drives cost and how it compares with digging.

How a cured-in-place liner goes into the pipe

1

Camera inspection first

A pipe camera shows cracks, roots, offsets, sags and the pipe material. The footage tells you whether lining is even possible.

2

Cleaning the line

A drain machine or hydro jetter removes roots, scale and debris so the liner bonds to a clean surface.

3

Preparing and inserting the liner

A felt or fiberglass tube is soaked in epoxy resin, then pulled or inverted into the pipe from an access point.

4

Inflating and curing

The tube is pressed against the pipe walls, then cured with hot water, steam or ultraviolet light until it hardens.

5

Reopening connections and a final camera check

A cutter reopens side connections, and a second camera run confirms the liner is smooth and sealed.

What lining can fix and what it cannot

A liner follows the shape of the pipe it goes into. That strength is also its limit.

Pipe conditionLining a good fit?Why
Root intrusion at jointsUsually yes, after cleaningThe liner seals joints so roots cannot enter
Hairline cracks or small holesUsually yesResin bonds over the defect
Minor corrosion in old clay or cast ironOften yesA new smooth wall replaces the rough one
Offset joints where pipes have shiftedDepends on how bigLarge steps can snag the liner or leave a ridge
Belly or sag holding waterNo, the liner follows the dipWater still collects in the low spot
Collapsed or crushed pipeNoThe liner cannot pass or has nothing to bond to

Only a camera view can place your pipe in one of these rows.

Lining versus digging the line out

Cured-in-place lining

  • Needs only one or two small access holes, so lawns, driveways and landscaping stay mostly intact
  • Can cover a long run in a single pass
  • Slightly reduces the inner diameter, which is rarely an issue for a household line
  • Fails to fix bellies and collapsed pipe

Open trench replacement

  • Replaces the pipe entirely, so any offset, belly or collapse gets corrected
  • Disturbs the yard, and may involve driveways, sidewalks and trees
  • Often the only option for a crushed line
  • Usually takes more labor and restoration work

What Chesterfield homeowners should check

Chesterfield note

The City of Chesterfield says the property owner is responsible for the lateral sewer service line, which is the pipe from the house to the main. The City's Residential Sanitary Sewer Lateral Repair Program covers certain lateral repairs after the line has been cabled by a licensed plumbing company or licensed drainlayer and the cabling did not clear the problem. Routine cabling is not reimbursable.

Whether lining qualifies under that program is not something we can promise. Ask the City's Department of Public Works whether your repair qualifies before you commit, because the City uses its own repair contractor for program repairs. Applications go by mail or in person with a cabling invoice, so keep the paperwork from any cabling visit.

Source types: city website, city sewer lateral program.

What drives the cost of sewer lining

Prices differ from house to house, so any number you hear without a camera inspection is a guess.

Length of the runA longer lateral takes more liner and more cure time.
Pipe diameter and materialLarger or older pipe, especially with heavy scale, takes more preparation.
Access pointsA clean-out makes work simpler, while a missing one means excavating a small access pit.
Cleaning and root removalA badly invaded line needs more jetting or cutting before the liner can go in.
Number of connectionsEach side line needs to be cut open and sealed after curing.

Homeowners policies commonly cover sudden accidental damage but not wear and maintenance, so check your policy. We give a clear price before work starts.

Protecting a lined pipe after the work is done

A new liner makes the inside of the pipe smooth, but it does not make the line immune to abuse. Grease, wipes and flushed paper towels still clog a lined pipe the same way they clog any other. Keep fats out of the kitchen drain and flush only toilet paper.

Ask your plumber to leave you the final camera footage, the liner product name and the date of the work. If you sell the house, that record helps the next owner. A camera run every few years on an older line also catches new trouble early, before a backup does.

Why a camera comes before any quote

A pipe camera pushed through the clean-out shows what is actually happening underground. Without it, a lining quote is a guess, and a collapsed section can turn an expected trenchless job into a dig. The footage also lets you see the pipe material and the exact distance to each defect.

Ask for a copy of the video or a still of each problem. Our sewer inspection visit does this, and if the camera finds a defect that cabling cannot fix, our sewer line repair team can talk through lining, spot repair or replacement. We also serve homes in Wildwood and Creve Coeur.

When lining is the wrong call

If the camera shows a belly, a collapse or a severe offset, lining will not correct it, and spending on a liner would be wasted. A very short damaged section is sometimes better handled by a spot repair. If a home is about to be sold or the line is tied to a larger renovation, a full replacement may make more sense.

Sewage in the house or a backup into the basement is an emergency, so call the 24/7 line and stay out of standing water. Keep fixtures from running while you wait.
