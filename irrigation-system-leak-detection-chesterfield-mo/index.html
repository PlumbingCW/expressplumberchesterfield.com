Irrigation System Leak Detection in Chesterfield, MO

How to spot a sprinkler leak from the lawn, the bill and the meter, and what to check at the valve box and backflow device.

Irrigation system leak detection in Chesterfield, MO starts with the symptoms you can see. One zone that stays soggy a day after it runs. A patch of grass that is greener than the rest. A spray head that barely reaches. Or a water bill that jumped in a month when the lawn did not seem to need more.

A buried leak wastes water and can undermine soil under a patio or walkway. The meter test below takes about ten minutes and tells you whether the leak is in the sprinkler system, in the house or in the service line.

The meter test with irrigation turned off

1

Stop all water use

Close every faucet, make sure the dishwasher and washing machine are idle and turn off the irrigation controller so no zone starts mid-test.

2

Read the meter

Open the meter lid and look at the small leak indicator, usually a triangle or a dial that spins with any flow. Write down the full reading.

3

Wait and watch

Wait at least 30 minutes without using any water. Read it again. A moving indicator or changed number means water is escaping somewhere.

4

Isolate the irrigation

Close the irrigation supply valve, then read the meter again. If the movement stops, the leak is in the sprinkler side. If it keeps moving, look at the house or the line from the meter.

5

Check the toilets

A silent toilet leak can mimic a sprinkler problem. Put a few drops of food coloring in each tank and wait twenty minutes.

What to look for in the valve box

The valve box is the green or black lid set flush in the lawn, and it holds the zone valves. Open it and look for standing water, a hissing valve, a loose wire connector or a cracked fitting. A zone valve that does not seal fully can leak slowly into the downstream line for hours after the zone stops.

Wear gloves and watch for insects. If a solenoid is the problem, turning it by hand a quarter turn often shows whether the valve opens and closes. If water keeps trickling with the solenoid off, the diaphragm or the valve body is the likely culprit.

Many valve boxes fill with mud and become a pool. Clear the water out before you look so you can see where fresh water enters.

Backflow device notes

The backflow preventer protects drinking water from garden chemicals and lawn runoff.

Where it sits

Usually on a short run of pipe near the house, either above ground or in a box. It has test cocks and a body that holds a check assembly.

What a leak means

A steady drip at the relief port can signal a debris-fouled check or a worn seal. This is not a simple tighten-and-forget job.

Why testing matters

Some utilities and municipalities require periodic testing of these devices. Ask the City or St. Louis County whether a test or permit applies to your system.

Freeze damage

A device left full of water can crack in winter. A cracked body leaks when you turn the system back on in spring.

Winter shutdown guidance for Chesterfield irrigation

Chesterfield note

Missouri American Water advises customers in cold weather to know where the main shutoff valve is, usually near the meter or where the line enters the house, and to test it. It also advises insulating exposed pipes and draining and turning off irrigation systems.

That matters here because a system left charged through a hard freeze can crack a valve, a backflow body or a lateral, and you may not see it until spring. Drain the lines, close the irrigation supply and leave the controller off for winter. If a pipe does freeze, shut off the main first, warm the area gently, never use open flame and restore water slowly while you check joints.

Source types: utility winter guidance.

What you can check yourself and what needs a locator

A homeowner can narrow the search a lot. Confirming and fixing a buried leak is slower without the right tools.

Homeowner checks

  • Run each zone separately and watch the heads
  • Do the meter test with irrigation off
  • Look in every valve box for water
  • Check for a stuck or clogged head
  • Lower the water pressure and see if the leak stops

When to bring in a plumber

  • The meter moves with every fixture off and the irrigation closed
  • A leak sits under a driveway or patio
  • You cannot tell which zone or line is leaking
  • The backflow device drips or needs testing
  • Wet soil or sinking ground near the foundation

How we find the leak

We start with the meter and a pressure gauge on a hose bib. If the irrigation side is the culprit, we close it off and test each zone. An acoustic leak detector picks up the sound of water escaping from a pressurized pipe, and a moisture meter confirms damp ground.

Our leak detection work narrows the spot before any digging, so the repair is small. For new valves, zone changes or a backflow upgrade, plumbing installation covers the connection work. Commercial and HOA properties with larger systems can ask about commercial plumbing service.
