The first minutes of a plumbing emergency

Most plumbing damage grows in the time between the first sign and the first action. A supply line that is open can put gallons on a floor every minute, and a sewage backup can reach finished flooring quickly. A few steps cost nothing and often decide how big the repair becomes.

Call the line right away when water cannot be stopped, sewage is coming up a floor drain or tub, there is no hot water in cold weather, or you smell gas. For a gas smell, leave the building first and call the gas utility or 911 from outside.

  • 1
    Shut off the waterTurn the main valve clockwise. It is usually near the water meter or where the line enters the house. A fixture with its own stop valve under the sink or behind the toilet can be closed alone.
  • 2
    Protect power and peopleIf water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, stay out. Switch off power only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water.
  • 3
    Stop using drainsWhen sewage backs up, do not run sinks, showers, dishwashers or washers. Every gallon you add has the same place to go.
  • 4
    Move what you canLift rugs, boxes and electronics off wet floors. Photos of the damage on your phone help later if you file an insurance claim.
  • 5
    Call the 24/7 lineHave the street address, a short description, and the main valve location ready. Say if anyone is elderly, ill or without water.

Plumbing services grouped by the problem you have

Every service below has its own page with symptoms, how the cause is found, and what drives cost. Start with the group that matches what you see.

Drains and sewers

A slow tub or gurgling toilet usually starts with drain cleaning, and greasy or root-packed lines may need hydro jetting. Main-line stoppages call for sewer line cleaning, a camera look through sewer inspection, and when the pipe itself has failed, sewer line repair.

Water heaters

No hot water or a leaking tank starts with water heater repair. When the unit is past saving, water heater replacement and water heater installation cover the swap, and a tankless water heater suits households that want endless hot water.

Leaks and pipes

A pipe that has split needs burst pipe repair fast. A hidden leak behind a wall or under a slab calls for leak detection, and a house with worn supply lines may be better served by repiping.

Fixtures

A running toilet or weak flush is a job for toilet repair. Drips and sputtering spouts fall under faucet repair, and a jammed or humming unit under the sink is covered by garbage disposal repair.

Planned work and protection

New fixtures, lines and appliances go through plumbing installation. For wet basements, sump pump installation and repair and basement waterproofing work together, and water softener and filtration address scale and taste.

Businesses

Restaurants, offices and retail units have bigger drain runs and tighter schedules. See commercial plumbing for what a business should have ready when it calls.

Chesterfield Valley, Gumbo Flats and why the ground matters

Chesterfield Valley, once known as Gumbo Flats, is low Missouri River floodplain on the north edge of the city. The Great Flood of 1993 put about 4,000 acres of it under 6 to 15 feet of water after the Monarch levee gave way. The Valley now sits behind the Monarch-Chesterfield Levee and is mostly office and retail.

For plumbing, low ground means basements and lower levels deserve a working sump pump and a backwater plan. Large commercial buildings in the Valley also carry big drain and backflow needs. If you own a building down there, ask about pump testing before storm season rather than after a wet night.

Roads, parks and landmarks we use to find you

Chesterfield is about 25 miles west of St. Louis, and the city is served by Interstate 64 and US 40, Olive Boulevard and Route 141. Landmarks help us picture the area, but a street address is what dispatch needs. Many subdivisions sit between the same two roads, so cross streets and a house number save time on the phone.

Faust Park, with its Butterfly House and historic village, and Chesterfield Mall are well known to everyone here, yet each is surrounded by many different streets. St. Luke's Hospital is a major employer, and shift workers often come home to a problem at odd hours. That is a practical reason the line stays open overnight.

Caulks Creek and Bonhomme Creek both run into the Missouri River in Chesterfield. After heavy rain, properties near creek corridors can see wet basements and strained sump pumps. If the pit is full and the pump will not start, that is a call-now situation.

Hard water and Chesterfield water heaters

Worth knowing

Missouri American Water supplies most of St. Louis County. Its 2021 water quality report for the St. Louis region lists hardness of 76 to 170 mg/L as calcium carbonate at Missouri River facilities, about 4.4 to 9.9 grains per gallon. Meramec River facilities came in at 66 to 148 mg/L, about 3.9 to 8.7 grains per gallon.

Hardness in that range builds scale on heating elements, in aerators and inside tankless heat exchangers. Ask Missouri American Water for its latest report and which plant serves your address, then decide whether a softener or a yearly tank flush makes sense.
