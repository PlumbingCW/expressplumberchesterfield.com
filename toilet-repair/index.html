Most toilet calls are not floods. They are a bowl that rocks, a damp ring around the base, a tank that refills every few minutes, or a flush that barely moves the water. Each one points to a different part, and a few point below the floor.

When a toilet overflow is an emergency

A bowl that rises toward the rim and keeps rising is an emergency, especially upstairs where water reaches the ceiling below within minutes. The same goes for sewage backing up through the toilet, or water seeping out from the base of a toilet on a finished floor. Do not keep flushing to test it. Every flush adds more water to a drain that is already blocked.

If sewage is coming up in a tub or floor drain as well, the blockage is in the main line, and sewer line cleaning is the fix.

  • 1

  • 2

  • 3
    Put towels at the base and move rugs, bath mats and anything electrical away from the wet area.
  • 4

  • 5

What your toilet is telling you

The symptom usually narrows the cause before anyone touches a tool. This is how we read the common ones.

A single plunge is fine for a simple clog. If two or three tries do nothing, the blockage is likely past the toilet and needs a drain machine.

Running toilets waste more than the noise suggests

A toilet that runs for a few seconds after every flush is annoying. One that runs all day can pass a lot of water without anyone noticing until the bill arrives. The usual culprits are a flapper that has gone soft, a chain that is too tight or too loose, or a fill valve whose seal is worn.

A simple dye test tells us which. We drop a few drops of food coloring in the tank, wait without flushing, and look at the bowl. Color in the bowl means the flapper is leaking. A clear bowl with a tank that still refills points at the overflow tube or the fill valve.

Flappers and fill valves are cheap parts and a short job. We replace them rather than adjust them when the rubber is brittle, because a patched flapper tends to fail again in a few months.

We check whether the leak is clean supply water, tank condensation or drain water. Each one has a different fix, and a damp towel under the supply line settles it fast.

3

4

Small parts get swapped in the tank. A leaking base means pulling the toilet, scraping the old seal and setting it on a fresh one.

5

Test and clean up

We flush several times, check the base and supply connections for drips and wipe the area down before we leave.

When the flange or the floor is the real problem

A wax ring is a small seal between the toilet and the drain flange. When it fails, water seeps out at the base, and replacing it is a routine part of toilet repair. But if a new seal leaks again within weeks, the problem is rarely the wax.

The flange is the ring bolted to the floor that the toilet sits on. If it is cracked, corroded or sitting below the finished floor after new tile, the seal cannot compress properly. Flange repair rings, spacers or a new flange fix those cases.

Long term leaks do more damage. Water that has soaked into the subfloor, sometimes traced with leak detection gear when the source is hidden, can leave it soft, and no seal will hold a toilet steady on rotted wood. We will tell you honestly if the floor needs carpentry before the toilet goes back, and we do not patch over it.

Repair the old toilet or put in a new one

Not every toilet is worth saving. The choice depends on the age of the bowl, what is failing and how often it has been fixed.

Repair makes sense when

  • The bowl and tank are uncracked and flush well
  • The problem is a flapper, fill valve, handle or supply line
  • The seal failed but the flange and floor are sound
  • You like the model and parts are still available

Replace makes sense when

  • There is a hairline crack in the bowl or tank
  • The toilet clogs repeatedly even with a clear drain
  • Parts are discontinued or the tank is stained and fragile
  • A flange or floor repair means pulling it anyway

Planning a new toilet installation

Buying the toilet is the easy part. Fit is what trips people up, so check these before you carry one home.

Rough-in distance

Measure from the finished wall behind the toilet to the center of the floor bolts. The usual distance is 12 inches, but 10 and 14 exist. A mismatch leaves the tank touching the wall or the bowl standing out.

Bowl shape and height

Elongated bowls sit longer than round ones and need extra room in front. Taller comfort height models are easier on knees but may not suit a child.

Supply valve and line

An old, stiff or leaking shutoff valve is replaced at the same visit. Reusing a tired valve is how a new install ends up leaking again.

Old toilet removal

We drain the old unit, lift it out, scrape the flange clean and check it before setting the new one on a fresh seal.

Permits and licensing for toilet installs

Worth knowing

Swapping a like-for-like toilet is routine, but moving one or adding a new bathroom is a different scale of work. Permit rules vary, so ask the City of Chesterfield or St. Louis County before work that may need a permit. Plumber licensing in Missouri is set locally, so confirm any plumber's licence with the building department where the job is. We also serve homes in Town and Country and across St. Louis County.

What not to do with a failing toilet

  • ✓
    Do not keep flushing a slow toilet to see if it clears. It can overflow in the middle of the test.
  • ✓
    Do not put a brick or bottle in the tank to save water. It can block the flapper from seating.
  • ✓
    Do not use a plunger with caustic cleaner standing in the bowl.
  • ✓
    Do not caulk around the base to hide a slow leak. Water trapped under the toilet rots the floor unseen.
  • ✓
    Do not over-tighten closet bolts. Porcelain cracks, and a cracked bowl means a new toilet.
