Plumbing installation in Chesterfield, MO covers a lot of ground, from a new kitchen sink to the drain and supply lines for a bathroom that did not exist last year. The right plan depends less on the fixture than on what is behind the wall. Where is the nearest drain, and how far is the supply?

We start by walking the space with you, then trace the supply and drain paths and tell you what the job will take before any pipe is cut.

Why the drain decides the plan

Supply pipe is flexible and forgiving. A drain is not. Waste lines depend on slope, usually a quarter inch of drop per foot, and every fixture needs a vent so the trap does not siphon dry. That is why a sink on the opposite wall from the existing stack can cost more than the sink itself.

On a remodel, the first question is where the existing drain and vent can be reached. In a basement with exposed joists this is simple. On a slab or under a finished floor, a drain connection may mean cutting concrete or running a lift station, and we tell you that before you buy the fixture.

New construction is easier because pipes go in before the walls close. Rough-in, a pressure test and inspection come first, then finish trim once drywall is up.

Adding a bathroom or a laundry hookup

These two projects raise the same early questions.

Where does the waste go

A full bath usually needs a 3 inch drain and a vent. If the house drain is below the new fixtures, an ejector pit and pump may be needed.

Can the supply keep up

A new bathroom adds demand. We check that the supply line and water heater can handle another shower at the same time.

Where are the shutoffs

A laundry hookup needs accessible stops, and the washer box should be installed so hoses are easy to close.

Does it need a permit

Added fixtures and moved lines often trigger review, so check before starting.

PEX supply lines against copper

For new supply runs, PEX is common in remodels, and copper is still used in some jobs.

PEX installation

  • Flexible, so fewer fittings in tight walls
  • Quicker to run through joists
  • Cannot be used outdoors in sunlight
  • Needs a manifold or tees planned in advance

Copper installation

  • Rigid and neat when exposed
  • Suits visible runs and some heat-exposed spots
  • More time to solder or press-fit
  • Material costs usually run higher

Gas smell or suspected gas leak: leave first

If you smell gas, the rotten egg odor added to natural gas, leave the building right away. Do not flip switches, use a phone inside, light anything or try to find the source. Call the gas utility or 911 from outside.

Gas line inspection and installation is regulated work with its own rules and testing, and this site has no separate gas page. We can talk through gas appliance connections and what a pressure test involves, but a suspected leak is a utility and fire department call before it is ever a plumbing call.

How an installation job runs

1

Walk and measure

We look at the space, find the nearest drain, vent and supply, and note any wall, joist or slab that complicates the run.

2

Review the plan and price

You get a plain explanation of the route, the materials and the price before work starts.

3

Permit check

We help you find out whether the City or St. Louis County needs to review the job.

4

Rough-in

Supply, drain and vent are run, and the system is pressure tested before anything is hidden behind a wall.

5

Finish and test

Fixtures are set, connections are checked for drips and drains are run to confirm flow.

What decides the price of an installation

Installing a sink is not the same as adding a bathroom. These factors move the number.

Distance to existing linesShort runs to a nearby stack are far cheaper than extending lines across a house.
AccessOpen joists or unfinished basements are simpler than slabs and finished walls.
Fixture and material choiceYour faucet, toilet and pipe selection set the material cost.
Venting and drain slopeIf code requires a new vent or extra fall, the work expands.
Permit and inspectionAdded fees and timing depend on local requirements.

Insurance rarely pays for planned improvements, though it may cover sudden damage from a fixture that fails later. Check your policy.

Permits and licensing in Chesterfield

Worth knowing

The City of Chesterfield contracts with St. Louis County for permitting and inspection, and the City's Department of Planning handles zoning approval. Permit rules vary, so ask the City or St. Louis County before work that may need a permit. Missouri sets plumber licensing locally, so confirm any plumber's licence with the building department where the job is.
