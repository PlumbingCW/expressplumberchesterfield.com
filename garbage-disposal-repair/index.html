Why a disposal hums but does not turn

A humming motor with a stuck blade means something is wedged between the grinding plate and the impellers. Fruit pits, bottle caps, bits of silverware and fibrous peels are common culprits. The motor draws power, but the flywheel cannot move, and the unit may trip its own overload.

Most disposals have a hex socket in the center of the underside. A quarter turn with a hex wrench from underneath can free the flywheel once the power is off. A reset button on the bottom, usually red, pops out when the motor overheats and can be pressed after a few minutes.

If the unit trips again right away, or the reset will not stay in, stop using it. A burned motor does not recover, and repeated resets only heat it more.

What should never go down the disposal

Disposals handle soft scraps and not much else. These items cause most of the service calls.

  • ✓
    Grease and cooking fatIt cools inside the drain and builds a lining that slows the whole line.
  • ✓
    Coffee grounds in bulkThey settle into a paste in the trap and bring on slow drains.
  • ✓
    Potato peels and stringy vegetablesCelery, corn husks and artichoke leaves wrap around the blades.
  • ✓
    Bones, fruit pits and shellsHard material dulls the blade and jams the plate.
  • ✓
    Rice, pasta and oatmealThey swell with water and clump in the trap.
  • ✓
    Non-food itemsUtensils, bottle caps, sponge scraps and twist ties can damage the unit or the drain beyond it.

When a disposal leak or backup is an emergency

A disposal that leaks steadily from its body, or one that sends sink water into a second basin or the dishwasher, needs attention right away. Water that reaches the electrical connection under the sink is a shock risk. Leaking food waste water also soaks the cabinet floor and swells particle board.

Where the leak is coming from

The location of the drip tells us the part, and each one has a different fix.

Leak locationWhat failedTypical repair
Around the sink flange at the topDried plumber's putty or loose mounting ringRemove, clean and reset the flange with fresh putty.
Bottom of the unit near the reset buttonCracked housing or failed internal sealReplace the disposal. The housing cannot be fixed.
Dishwasher hose connectionLoose clamp or aged hoseReplace the clamp or hose and check the knockout opening.
Drain pipe outlet on the sideLoose gasket or flange boltsReseat the gasket and tighten the outlet flange.
Trap joints beyond the disposalWorn washers or misaligned slip nutsRealign and replace washers.

A leak from the bottom or the body almost always means replacement. A leak from the top flange or a pipe joint is a much smaller repair.

Repair the disposal or replace it

Repair usually works when

  • The blades are free after a hex wrench turn
  • The leak is at the flange, gasket or trap
  • The reset button works and holds
  • The unit is otherwise quiet and strong

Replace when

  • The housing leaks near the bottom
  • The motor hums, trips and will not recover
  • The blades are worn and grinding is slow
  • Rust or corrosion shows inside the grinding chamber

How a disposal installation goes

A swap takes less than a morning in most kitchens, but the order matters.

1

Cut power and disconnect

We turn off the circuit, unplug or disconnect the old unit and detach the drain, dishwasher hose and mounting ring.

2

Prepare the sink flange

The sink opening is cleaned and a new ring of plumber's putty or a gasket sets the flange.

3

Handle the knockout

If a dishwasher connects, the plug inside the new disposal's side inlet must be knocked out first. Skipping it blocks the dishwasher drain.

4

Connect power

A cord plugs into an outlet under the sink, or the unit is wired to its own switch. We check the circuit before connecting.

5

Mount and run it

We lock the unit onto the mounting ring, reconnect the drain and dishwasher line, then run water and test for leaks.

Electrical and permit questions

Worth knowing

A disposal is a plumbing and an electrical item. Missouri sets plumber licensing locally, so confirm any contractor's licence with the building department where the job is. Permit rules vary, so ask the City of Chesterfield or St. Louis County before work that may need a permit or new wiring.

The dishwasher connection and air gap

A dishwasher usually drains through the disposal inlet or an air gap on the countertop. If the disposal is clogged, dishwasher water can back up into the dishwasher itself. A clean dishwasher drain hose with a high loop helps too.

If the whole sink line is slow, the problem is beyond the disposal. A drain cleaning visit clears the branch, and a stubborn line may need hydro jetting. We serve kitchens in Wildwood as well as Chesterfield.
