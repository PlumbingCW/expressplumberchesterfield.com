When a business plumbing problem is an emergency

Water on a commercial floor is a safety and liability problem, not only a repair. Standing water makes slips likely, and wet carpet and drywall can grow mold in a couple of days. A backed-up floor drain in a food area can also close a kitchen for the night.

We take after-hours calls on the 24/7 line and book same-day appointments when available. The first call should tell us what the water is, where it is coming from and where the shutoffs are.

  • 1
    Shut off the nearest fixture valve, or the building main if you cannot tell where the water starts.
  • 2
    Keep people out of the wet area and put out wet floor signs.
  • 3
    Move stock, electronics and paper records away from the water, if it is safe to do so.
  • 4
    If wiring or equipment is in the water, stay clear and call the electrician or utility.
  • 5
    Photograph the damage for the owner and the insurance claim, then call us.

Problems that bring businesses to the phone

Floor drains that overflow

Floor drains in kitchens, mechanical rooms and garages back up when grease, debris or a blocked branch line stops flow. Water arriving from a drain after rain suggests a different issue than one that backs up after a busy lunch.

Hidden leaks

A rising water bill, a stained ceiling tile or a damp slab can mean a supply leak. Finding the exact spot saves cutting extra holes.

Restroom failures

Flushometer valves, clogged mains and leaking urinals shut down fixtures at the worst moments.

Grease trap and interceptor trouble

Slow drains, odors and overflow near kitchens often trace to a full trap.

Backflow device questions

Testing, failed assemblies and notices from the water provider come on a schedule that is rarely convenient.

Leak detection for a building, and what it can and cannot do

Detection starts with the water meter. If the meter keeps moving after every fixture is shut off, water is leaking somewhere. A moisture meter, thermal imaging, an acoustic listening device and a pressure test on isolated branches narrow it down.

Honestly, these tools tell us where a leak probably is, not always what is wrong with it. A large building with several risers, a slab and multiple tenants can take more than one visit, and some leaks sit under flooring where finding them means opening a floor.

We tell you when the evidence is thin, and we do not suggest cutting concrete on a hunch. For the same process at a smaller scale, see our leak detection service.

Grease trap cleaning for restaurants and food service

A grease trap catches fats and oils before they reach the sewer. When the trap fills, grease reaches the line, builds up and causes overflow. Cleaning means pumping out the contents and scraping the baffles and walls.

How often depends on volume, menu and trap size. A busy fryer-heavy kitchen needs service far more often than a small café. Ask the City or your landlord whether your property has a required cleaning schedule, since it is set locally.

Chesterfield Valley and large buildings

Worth knowing

Chesterfield Valley, once called Gumbo Flats, is low-lying Missouri River floodplain that was farmland before it became office and retail. Chesterfield Commons and other retail in the Valley are typical of that growth, though we do not name any business as a customer. Large buildings there often have big drains, grease interceptors and backflow devices, and their size makes a lingering leak or a blocked main more expensive.

Since the Valley sits low, ask your property manager about sump pumps and floor drain backflow before heavy rain.

Backflow testing is planned work

A backflow preventer keeps dirty water from flowing back into the drinking supply. Many commercial properties have them on fire lines, irrigation and the main water service. Testing rules, schedules and approved testers come from the property's water utility or the City, so ask them what your property needs.

We can inspect and test assemblies where the rules allow it and repair a failed device. Whatever the rules say, a failed test is something to fix soon, and we explain what was found and the options before we start.
