Will Coconut Oil Clog Drains in Chesterfield, MO?

Yes, it can. Coconut oil turns solid at room temperature, so it coats the inside of a drain and catches whatever else follows.

Coconut oil will clog drains in Chesterfield, MO, and it does so faster than most cooking oils. It melts at about 76 degrees, which is roughly room temperature in many kitchens. Poured down a sink warm or melted, it cools in the pipe and hardens into a waxy coat.

One spoonful rarely causes trouble. Years of small amounts build a layer that narrows the pipe until food scraps and hair stick to it. This guide shows what happens inside, what to do with fats instead and how to tell a kitchen drain clog from a line problem.

What happens to coconut oil inside a pipe

Hot water from the tap carries melted oil through the trap and into the drain line. The pipe wall is cooler than the oil, so the oil cools on contact and sticks. The drain line is often not heated and sits in a wall, a crawl space or under a slab.

Each wash leaves another thin film. Over weeks the film thickens, grabs coffee grounds, rice and soap scum and turns into a gray, soft plug. Hot water can melt it for a moment, but the oil moves down the line and sets again farther away.

Coconut oil is more saturated than olive or vegetable oil, so it hardens at warmer temperatures. That makes it a worse choice for a drain than liquid oils, though none of them belong there.

Fats that clog versus fats that are less likely to

No cooking fat is safe for a drain. Some are simply worse.

Solidify quickly in pipes

  • Coconut oil
  • Butter and ghee
  • Bacon grease and lard
  • Shortening
  • Palm oil

Stay liquid longer, still not drain safe

  • Olive oil
  • Canola and vegetable oil
  • Sesame oil
  • Salad dressings
  • Nut oils

Signs a grease buildup is forming

Slow draining kitchen sink

Water sits for a minute after you pull the stopper and clears in a lazy swirl.

Gurgling after the sink empties

Air is fighting through a partial blockage in the line.

A sour or rancid smell

Old fat in the trap and pipe breaks down and smells.

Other fixtures react

The dishwasher or a nearby sink drains slowly or backs up with it.

Repeat clogs after cleaning

A plug that returns in a few weeks usually means a greasy coat is still on the walls.

What to do instead of pouring it down the drain

1

Let it cool and harden

Pour fat into a heat-safe jar or an old can and let it set.

2

Wipe pans first

Use a paper towel to wipe out the pan before washing. Put the towel in the trash.

3

Bag it for the trash

When the container is full, seal it and throw it away with household trash.

4

Use a sink strainer

A basket strainer keeps food bits out so they cannot catch on any film that remains.

5

Keep it out of the disposal

A garbage disposal grinds food but does not stop fat from coating the pipe.

Kitchen clog or a bigger line problem?

A grease clog is usually local to one fixture. A main line problem affects more of the house.

Chemical drain openers can burn skin, damage old pipe and make the next repair more dangerous. Skip them if the drain is fully blocked.

How a plumber finds a grease clog and clears it

The first step is to learn how far the problem reaches. We ask which fixtures drain slowly, then run a drain machine until the cutter meets the blockage. The feel of the cable often tells us whether it is soft grease, a hard plug or a root mass.

For a greasy line, a cutter alone can leave a thin film on the wall, which regrows into a new plug. A hydro jetter uses high pressure water to strip the pipe, and a pipe camera shows whether the wall is clean and the line is intact afterward.

If the camera shows a sag, a crack or a root entry, no amount of cleaning will fix it, and repair is the honest next step. A grease habit and a damaged pipe often show up together.

Chesterfield sewer laterals and the City repair program

Chesterfield note

The City of Chesterfield says the property owner is responsible for the lateral sewer service line. The City runs a Residential Sanitary Sewer Lateral Repair Program that can cover certain lateral repairs up to $15,000, but only after a licensed plumbing company or licensed drainlayer has cabled the line and it did not clear the problem.

Routine cabling is not reimbursable, and the owner pays for the cabling and any cost above the cap. Keep the cabling invoice, because applications need it. Ask the City's Department of Public Works whether your repair qualifies. The program also has a non-refundable $200 application fee.

Kitchen habits that keep fat out of the line

  • ✓
    Scrape plates into the trash before rinsing, including butter and sauce.
  • ✓
    Keep a small lidded jar by the stove for cooking oil and bacon fat.
  • ✓
    Wipe greasy pans and baking sheets with a paper towel before they reach the sink.
  • ✓
    Run hot water for a short time after washing a heavy load of dishes to move residue along.
  • ✓
    Do not rely on enzyme or bacteria products to replace cleaning once a line is slow.
  • ✓
    Empty the sink strainer basket daily.
