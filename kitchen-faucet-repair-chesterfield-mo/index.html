Kitchen Faucet Repair in Chesterfield, MO

Work out whether the leak is at the spout, the base or the handle, fix what is cheap and know when a new faucet makes more sense.

A kitchen faucet that drips, sprays sideways or leaks under the sink usually has a small part wearing out. Kitchen faucets repair in Chesterfield, MO often means a cartridge, an aerator or a supply hose, and most are within reach of a homeowner with a wrench and a flashlight.

The key is figuring out where the water comes from. A leaky spout, a wet base and a drip from the handle each point to a different part. Start by shutting off the water under the sink, then follow the steps below.

Where the leak shows up and what it usually is

Dry the faucet, then run it and watch where the first drop appears.

Where water appearsLikely partTypical fix
Drip from the spout when offWorn cartridge, ball or washer seatReplace the cartridge or seals
Water around the base of the spoutWorn O-rings on a swivel spoutReplace the O-rings and grease them
Leak at the handleLoose cap, worn cartridge seal or packingTighten, then replace the seal or cartridge
Weak or splitting sprayClogged aerator or sprayer headSoak or clean the aerator
Puddle under the sinkSupply hose, shutoff valve or fittingTighten or replace the hose or valve
Pull-down spray hose leaksWorn hose or a failed quick-connectReplace the hose

Brand and model change the exact part, so take the old one with you or note the model on the faucet body.

Shut it off and take it apart

1

Find the shutoff valves

Look under the sink for two small valves, one for hot and one for cold. Turn each clockwise until snug.

2

Open the faucet to release pressure

Turn the handle on to drain what is left in the line. Keep the drain stopped so small parts do not fall in.

3

Remove the handle

Look for a set screw under a decorative cap or behind the handle. Lift it off and set the screw aside.

4

Pull the cartridge or stem

Take out the retaining nut or clip and pull the cartridge straight up. Compare it to the new one before installing.

5

Clean and reassemble

Wipe the seat, grease the new seals lightly with plumber's grease and reverse the steps. Turn the water on slowly.

What Chesterfield water does to faucets and what to do after repairs

Chesterfield note

Missouri American Water supplies most of St. Louis County. Its 2021 St. Louis region report put hardness at 76 to 170 mg/L at the Missouri River facilities and 66 to 148 mg/L at the Meramec River facilities, or roughly 3.9 to 9.9 grains per gallon. Water in that range leaves scale in aerators and on cartridges, which shortens their life.

The same report advises flushing a tap 30 seconds to 2 minutes after the water has sat for hours before you drink or cook with it. Do that after any faucet repair, since fresh work can stir up debris. Ask Missouri American Water for the latest report and which plant serves your address.

Quick fixes you can try first

Clean the aerator

Unscrew the tip, rinse out grit and soak it in vinegar for an hour if it is crusty. Reinstall hand tight.

Tighten the handle or cap

A loose handle can leak or feel sloppy. Snug the set screw and nut but do not force them.

Replace the O-rings

A swivel spout leaks at the base when its rings dry out and flatten. New rings and a thin film of grease often stop it.

Swap the supply hose

Flexible hoses under the sink wear out. Replace both at once with new braided hoses of the right length.

Check the shutoff valves

If a valve drips at its stem, a quarter turn of the packing nut may stop it. If not, replace the valve.

Repair or replace the faucet

Parts are cheap, but time and uncertainty add up on an old faucet.

Repair when

  • The faucet is a common model with parts available
  • Only one part is worn
  • The finish and body are in good shape
  • Hard water scale is the main issue
  • You like the faucet and its height

Replace when

  • The body is cracked or corroded
  • Parts are discontinued
  • You have patched it more than once
  • The finish is flaking or pitted
  • You want a pull-down sprayer or a different layout

Finding a leak that hides under the sink

Not every wet cabinet comes from the faucet. Dry the cabinet floor and the underside of the sink, then lay a dry paper towel under each connection. After a minute of running the tap, check which spot is wet first.

A drip on the supply connection at the shutoff valve means the nut or washer is loose or worn. A drip on the hose body means the braided hose is failing and should be replaced. Water at the sink drain trap is a separate problem and has nothing to do with the faucet.

A moisture meter can find damp wood in the cabinet that you cannot see. If the cabinet floor is soft or swollen, the leak has been going for a while and should be addressed quickly, since mold follows moisture.

Know when to stop and call a plumber

  • ✓
    A shutoff valve under the sink will not close fully.
  • ✓
    The valve handle breaks off or spins without turning the stem.
  • ✓
    Water seeps from the wall or the cabinet floor after you close the valve.
  • ✓
    The faucet body is corroded and the nuts will not budge.
  • ✓
    You are not sure which part is leaking.
  • ✓
    A leak is close to electrical outlets, in which case keep clear and shut the power off only if the panel is dry.

What drives the cost of a kitchen faucet repair

Parts alone are a small share. Time, access and condition decide the rest.

Faucet typeA single-handle cartridge faucet is quick. A pull-down or touchless model takes more time.
Part availabilityCommon cartridges are easy to find, but discontinued parts may mean replacement.
Shutoff valve conditionA failed valve adds work before the faucet can be fixed.
Scale and corrosionHeavy buildup slows disassembly and can break parts.
Sink and cabinet accessTight cabinets or undermount sinks take longer.

Homeowners policies typically cover sudden accidental water damage but not gradual leaks or wear. Document the leak and call your insurer if the cabinet or floor got wet.
