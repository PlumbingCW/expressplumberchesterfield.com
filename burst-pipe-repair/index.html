Burst pipe flooding right now: the first 60 seconds

Every minute with the water running adds gallons to the floor. These steps come before the phone call, and they are listed in the order that protects the most. After that, call the 24/7 line and we will plan a same-day or after-hours visit.

  • 1
    Shut off the main valveIt is usually near the water meter or where the line enters the house. Turn a ball valve a quarter turn, or a round gate valve clockwise until it stops.
  • 2
    Kill power to wet areas only if it is safeIf water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, stay out. Shut off the breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water.
  • 3
    Open a faucet at the lowest pointThis drains pressure from the line and slows the leak. Turn off the water heater too, so it does not run dry.
  • 4
    Move electronics, rugs and papersLift what you can onto tables or into dry rooms. Water spreads along flooring faster than most people expect.
  • 5
    Photograph everythingTake dated photos or video of the break, the water and the damage before cleanup. Your insurer will ask for them.

How a cracked or broken pipe shows itself

Not every break is a fountain. These quieter signs are often the first report we get.

A damp ceiling stain that keeps growing

A slow drip above a ceiling or inside a wall travels along framing before it shows.

A water meter that keeps moving with everything off

If every fixture is closed and the meter still turns, water is leaving somewhere underground or behind a wall.

Low pressure at every tap

A sudden drop at all fixtures can mean a split on the supply line or a failing main valve.

A warm spot on the floor

A hot-water line leaking under a slab or in a floor can heat the surface above it.

A hissing or running sound with no fixture on

Pressurized water escaping makes a steady noise in the wall or crawlspace.

If the signs are quiet and the source is hidden, pinpointing the leak comes first. Our leak detection page explains how listening gear and thermal tools find it before anything is opened.

Which pipe broke and how it is usually repaired

The fix depends on the pipe material and where it failed. Here is how common cases are handled.

Pipe or partCommon failureTypical repair
Copper supply linePinhole from corrosion, split from freezing, or a failed solder jointCut out the damaged length and sweat in a new section, or use compression or push fittings
PEX supply lineSplit from freezing or a crimp ring that has slippedCut out the damaged stretch and join with new fittings
PVC or ABS drain pipeCrack from impact, a loose slip joint or a hairline splitCut out the length and glue in a new section with couplings
Galvanized steel supplyPinhole leaks along a rusted pipeReplace the section, and consider replacing more as the rest is likely to follow
Shutoff or stop valvePacking leak, seized handle or cracked bodyReplace the valve, then test the shutoff

Copper pipe repair and when to stop patching

Copper fails in two ways. Cold splits a pipe lengthwise, and corrosion eats pinholes through a thin wall. A single split from one freeze is a good candidate for a clean section replacement. A run covered in green spots and multiple pinholes is another story. Patching one hole often moves the pressure to the next weak point.

If you have fixed leaks in the same line more than once, a repair bill adds up quickly. That is the time to talk about repiping the affected run rather than chasing each failure. We say plainly when a patch is the right call and when it is only a delay.

Broken supply valves and drain pipe repair

The valve that fails is often the one meant to stop the flood. Old stop valves under sinks and behind toilets seize or leak at the stem, and a main shutoff that will not close is its own emergency. If the main will not turn, call us and ask Missouri American Water how to shut off at the meter.

Drain pipe repair is a different job. A cracked drain line leaks only when the fixture drains, which makes the stain appear in odd places. A cracked or broken drain pipe is cut out and replaced with new pipe. If the leak actually came from a clogged line forcing water out of a joint, drain cleaning comes first.

Frozen pipes: thaw gently, restore slowly

Missouri American Water's winter guidance is clear on the order of events. Following it protects the pipe and the house.

1

Shut off the main first

Do this before thawing anything. A frozen pipe may already be split, and thawing it turns a hidden crack into a spray.

2

Warm the air around the pipe gently

Open cabinet doors under sinks and raise the room temperature. Never use an open flame, and never leave a space heater unattended.

3

Open the nearest faucet

As the ice melts, water and steam need somewhere to go.

4

Restore water slowly

Open the main a little at a time and check every joint and the thawed length for leaks.

5

Call if a pipe is split or you cannot reach it

A split in a wall or crawlspace is a repair, not a thaw. Our 24/7 line takes frozen pipe breaks.

Prevention follows the same guidance. Know where the main shutoff is and test it, insulate exposed pipes in crawl spaces, garages, basements and along exterior walls, drain and turn off irrigation, and keep the thermostat at 55 or higher when you are away.

What decides the cost of a burst pipe repair

Costs vary because burst pipes vary. We quote the price before work starts, and these factors do most of the moving.

Where the pipe isA visible break under a sink is quick. A break behind finished drywall or under a slab adds access work.
Pipe material and sizeCopper, PEX and PVC use different parts and methods, and larger pipe costs more to replace.
Extent of the damageOne split is a short fix. A freeze that cracked several fittings takes longer.
Opening and patching wallsDrywall, trim and paint repair after the pipe is fixed are usually separate from the plumbing.
Time of the callSame-day daytime visits fit our schedule. After-hours and weekend dispatch costs more because a crew is held ready.

Insurance note: homeowners policies commonly cover sudden accidental water damage, including a pipe that suddenly bursts, but not wear, corrosion or long-term seepage. Read your policy, document the damage, and ask your insurer what it needs before repairs begin.
