Fixture problems we see most

Different fixtures fail in different ways. Matching the symptom to the fixture saves a wasted trip.

Kitchen faucet drips from the spout

An internal cartridge, washer or seat has worn. The sprayer hose under the sink can also weep and be mistaken for the spout.

Cabinet floor is wet

A cracked supply line, a loose compression nut or a leaking drain trap. A dry paper towel run along each joint shows which one is wet.

Shower head or tub spout keeps dripping

Usually the valve cartridge behind the handle, not the showerhead. A showerhead that only sprays sideways is clogged with mineral scale.

Handle is loose or stiff

Worn stem, corroded cartridge or a set screw that backed out. Sometimes the valve body itself is pitted.

Low flow from one faucet only

Clogged aerator or a partially closed angle stop. If every faucet is low, the issue is the building supply instead.

When a shutoff valve will not close, it is an emergency

The worst fixture failures are the ones where you cannot stop the water. An angle stop that spins without closing, a supply line that has split, or a tub valve that sprays when the handle is off all count. Water at that pressure can empty a vanity into a basement in a few minutes.

We carry replacement stops and braided lines on the truck and can shut the water at the main while we work. Same-day and after-hours calls go through the 24/7 dispatch line, and we explain the repair and price before we cut anything.

Cartridge, washer, ceramic disc: how your faucet is built

Knowing the style tells us what wears out and whether repair is worth it. Many homes have more than one type.

Washer (compression) style

  • Two handles, each with a rubber washer and seat
  • Drip usually means the washer or seat is worn
  • Cheap to repair and easy to rebuild
  • Stems can seize in hard water scale

Cartridge or disc style

  • One lever or two handles with a sealed cartridge
  • Drip or stiff handle means replace the cartridge
  • Fast swap, but parts are brand specific
  • Ceramic discs last long and fail from grit

Shower valves and tub faucets behind the wall

A shower that drips after you turn it off is usually a worn cartridge or seat in the valve. A tub spout that leaks around the wall can be a failed diverter or a loose seal. Most of these are reachable by taking off the handle and trim without opening the tile.

When the valve body itself is corroded, or the leak shows up on the ceiling below, the repair is bigger. We may need an access panel, usually from the back side of the wall, and we will tell you where we need to cut before we cut.

If the wall is damp but you cannot tell where it comes from, a moisture meter and a pressure test on the shower branch separate a valve leak from a pipe leak. Hidden problems may call for leak detection before we open anything.

Hard water leaves marks on Chesterfield fixtures

Worth knowing

Missouri American Water supplies most of St. Louis County, and its 2021 water quality report listed hardness of 76 to 170 mg/L for Missouri River facilities, roughly 4.4 to 9.9 grains per gallon. That is moderately hard water. In practice it leaves white scale on aerators and showerheads, and stiff handles over time.

Soak a clogged aerator in vinegar for an hour and the flow often returns. Ask Missouri American Water for the latest report and which plant serves your address, since the numbers differ by source.

What happens when we come out for a fixture leak

1

Stop the water

We close the angle stop, or the main if the stop fails, and soak up what we can so we can see the actual joint.

2

Trace the drip

Dry each connection, then run the water and watch. A wet line at the valve points to the cartridge, while a wet trap points to the drain side.

3

Show you the options

We tell you whether a cartridge, a seat or the whole faucet is the smarter fix, and we give you the price before we start.

4

Repair or replace

We install the part or the new fixture, replace stiff shutoff valves while the area is open and reconnect the drain.

5

Run it and check

We run hot and cold, check for drips under the sink and leave the area dry.
