Chesterfield Valley is the flat commercial district on the Missouri River floodplain, reached from Interstate 64 and Chesterfield Airport Road. If you manage a restaurant, a retail unit or an office and need a plumber in Chesterfield Valley, MO, this page explains how those calls work. The Valley is a business district, so most calls start with restrooms, floor drains, kitchens and water heaters rather than family homes.

Everything stated as fact below comes from the City of Chesterfield or published reporting. Anything about the condition of one building is something a plumber can only learn by looking at it.

What the Valley is, and why that shapes a plumbing call

The Valley was farmland before the 1993 flood. The City says about 240 businesses operated there before that flood. Its fact sheet counts 909 licensed businesses in the Valley as of July 2021, which is 43.9 percent of all licensed businesses in Chesterfield.

The same fact sheet reports nearly 9 million square feet of development in 2021, up from 3.1 million square feet before 1993. It also lists 259 retail and restaurant operations, including 65 of the city's 133 restaurants. For a plumber, that means calls come from tenants and property managers who have staff, customers and a lease to think about.

The levee, the drainage work and what they do not cover

The City's fact sheet says the Valley's tax increment financing district paid for $21.27 million of levee work, $9.77 million of stormwater drainage and $10.853 million of sanitary and water utilities. St. Louis Magazine reports that the upgrades raised protection from the 100-year to the 500-year flood level.

That protection is aimed at river flooding. It does not stop a failed supply line, a clogged floor drain or a sewer backup inside a building, and those are plumbing problems with plumbing fixes. We do not claim any building here is more or less likely to have trouble than another.

Jobs a Valley business is most likely to call about

These are general examples of commercial calls, not a count of Valley jobs.

Stoppages and slow drains

A stoppage in a restroom block or kitchen line can close a business for the day. Drain cleaning clears the blockage, and hydro jetting scrubs the pipe wall when cable work keeps coming back.

Hidden leaks

A wet ceiling tile or a rising water bill can point to a leak behind a wall. Leak detection uses listening gear and pressure checks to find it before anyone opens a wall.

Water heaters for staff and customers

A heater that serves a kitchen or a restroom block works hard. Water heater replacement covers the swap, and the vent, drain pan and expansion tank details depend on the building.

Grease traps and building-wide work

Grease traps, floor drains and larger fixture counts fall under commercial plumbing. Tell the dispatcher what the space is used for and when the trap was last cleaned.
