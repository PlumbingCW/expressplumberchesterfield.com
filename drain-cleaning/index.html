When a clogged drain is an emergency

Most slow drains can wait for a morning appointment. A few cannot. Sewage rising in a floor drain or the lowest tub, water spreading toward a finished basement, or a house where nothing drains at all should be treated as an emergency. Wastewater carries bacteria, and every flush or load of laundry adds more to the floor.

Call the 24/7 line for these. We schedule same-day visits when the calendar allows and dispatch after hours for backups that cannot sit overnight. Fixture clog or main-line problem: what backs up first

The order in which drains misbehave tells you more than any single slow sink. Plumbers read the house like a map, starting from the lowest fixture and working up. Use this as a rough guide before you call.

Gurgling is air being pulled through a trap because water cannot move past a blockage. One gurgling fixture is a local clue. Gurgling in several rooms is a main-line clue.

What you can safely try before the truck arrives

Some clogs are honest DIY jobs, and paying for a visit on a hair-filled tub is a waste. Others get worse when handled wrong. These steps are low risk for a single slow fixture.

  • ✓
    Use a flat-bottom plungerSeal the overflow opening with a wet rag first, then plunge a tub or sink with steady strokes for about a minute.
  • ✓
    Pull the stopper or strainerHair wraps around the stopper arm and collects just below it. A plastic barbed strip from a hardware store pulls most of it out.
  • ✓
    Clean the P-trapPut a bucket under the curved pipe, loosen the slip nuts by hand and rinse the trap out. Tighten the nuts snugly, then run water and look for drips.
  • ✓
    Flush with hot tap waterHot tap water helps with light soap and grease film. Boiling water can soften PVC joints and crack porcelain, so skip it.
  • ✓
    Stop at the first sign of a bigger problemIf a second fixture starts gurgling or the water level climbs when you plunge, put the plunger down and call.

Why a kitchen sink drain clog keeps returning

Kitchen drains clog differently from bathroom drains. Cooking grease cools inside the pipe wall and traps coffee grounds, rice and food scraps against it. The line narrows a little each week until a double sink holds water after one load of dishes. Plunging may open a small channel, but the film stays behind.

A garbage disposal adds another stop. Ground food packs into the first bend below it, and the dishwasher hose often ties in right there. Dirty water left in the dishwasher tub usually means a clog at that junction. A cable run through the kitchen branch removes the pack and scrapes the walls. If the sink was clearing and slowing again every month, ask us about a hydro jetting pass, since hydro jetting scours grease that a cable only pokes through.

Prevention is dull but real. Wipe greasy pans with a paper towel before washing, bin the paper towel, and run hot water for 20 seconds after the dishes. Fibrous scraps such as celery strings and potato peels are poor disposal feed.

If the disposal itself hums, jams or leaks, that is a separate repair and has its own page for garbage disposal repair.

Snaking, jetting or a camera: which fits which clog

People call it all drain cleaning, but there are three different tools and each answers a different question. Picking the wrong one means paying twice.

Snake or cable first when

  • One fixture is blocked and the others run normally
  • The blockage is likely hair, a toy, a wipe or a soft plug close to the fixture
  • You need the drain open tonight and a deeper look can wait
  • A cleanout is within reach and the line is short

Jet or camera first when

  • The same drain has been cabled before and clogged again within weeks
  • A cable stops at the same spot or comes back with roots or heavy grease
  • Several fixtures are involved and the main line is suspected
  • You are buying or selling the house and want to know the pipe condition

A cable cuts a hole through a clog. Jetting washes the pipe wall clean, and a camera shows what the pipe actually looks like. If a cable keeps stopping short, a sewer camera inspection shows whether the line is cracked, offset or just coated, and that decides everything after.

How a drain visit goes from first look to open pipe

1

Map the symptoms

We ask which fixtures are slow, which gurgle, and what changed recently. This takes two minutes and often points to the branch before anyone opens a tool bag.

2

Find access

A cleanout, a removed toilet, a sink trap or a roof vent gives the machine a way in. A cleanout cap in the yard or basement is the best door.

3

Run the right machine

A hand cable suits a sink branch. A drum machine with a cutting head suits a larger drain. Either is run slowly so it does not snag and bend in a cracked pipe.

4

Test the flow

After clearing, we run water through the fixture and the neighbors on the same branch and watch for gurgling or slow drainage.

5

Explain what came out

Hair, grease, roots and scale each tell a different story. We say what we pulled and whether a repeat is likely, then give the price before any extra work.

Yard drains and storm drains around the house

Not every blocked drain is indoors. Chesterfield lots with buried downspout lines, patio catch basins or a low yard drain collect leaves, silt and mulch. After a heavy rain, standing water over a grate usually means the pipe beneath is partly full of debris. A garden hose and a short cable often clears it.

If the line stays full after the debris is gone, a section may be crushed, offset or full of roots, and cabling will not fix that. We would look inside with a camera rather than keep cleaning a pipe that cannot flow. Drains that connect to a street storm system or a neighbor's property raise ownership questions, so ask the City before assuming the whole run is yours.

A drain line leak is not a clog

Water stains on a ceiling under a bathroom, or a damp spot on a basement wall below a shower, can come from a leaking drain line rather than a supply pipe. Drain leaks usually show only when the fixture is used, which makes them easy to miss. A clogged line can also push water out through a loose joint.

The test is simple. Run only that fixture, watch the stain, and compare with the supply side. If drainage is normal and the stain still grows, the problem is a joint or crack, and it moves into pipe repair rather than cleaning.

What decides the cost of drain cleaning

Drain cleaning ranges from a quick cable run to a long machine job with a camera follow-up. We quote the price before the work starts so you can decide with the facts in front of you. These are the factors that move it.

Where the blockage sitsA trap or short sink branch is quick. A clog 40 feet down a branch costs more time and cable.
What the blockage isSoft hair is simple. Packed grease, root mass and mineral scale need heavier cutting heads or water.
Access to the pipeA clean outdoor cleanout saves time. If a toilet has to be pulled to reach the line, that adds labor and a new wax ring.

Follow-up neededA camera look or a second jetting pass after cabling adds time but can save a repeat clog.

Who owns the line from the house to the street

Worth knowing

The City of Chesterfield says the property owner is responsible for the lateral sewer service line. That matters when a cable run cannot clear the problem. The City's Residential Sanitary Sewer Lateral Repair Program can cover certain lateral repairs up to $15,000, but only after a licensed plumbing company or licensed drainlayer has cabled the line and it did not clear.

Routine cabling is not reimbursable, and the owner pays the cabling invoice and anything above the cap. The application costs a non-refundable $200 and goes in by mail or in person with the cabling invoice, last year's tax receipt showing the sewer lateral fee and a notarized hold-harmless agreement. Keep your invoice, and ask the City's Department of Public Works whether your repair qualifies. If the line proves broken, the next page is sewer line repair.
