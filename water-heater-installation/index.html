A planned installation is a different job from an emergency swap. You are choosing where the heater goes, what fuel it burns, how it vents, and what protects the floor if it ever leaks. Signs it is time to plan an installation

You do not need a leak to start planning. These situations call for a design decision, not a part swap.

A remodel changes the layout

A finished basement, a new addition or a bathroom moved to the other side of the house can leave the heater far from the fixtures, so hot water takes minutes to arrive.

The household has outgrown the tank

More people, a soaking tub or a second shower running at the same time means the old capacity no longer fits.

The unit sits somewhere it should not

Heaters tucked in a cramped closet, or located where water would reach finished floors with no drain, are worth moving.

Moving to a different fuel

Gas to electric, electric to gas or to a heat pump model each changes the venting, the circuit and the plan.

New construction or a rebuilt home

Rough-in decisions made early decide where the vent, drain and electrical run, and cost less before walls close.

Tank, tankless or hybrid: choosing before you install

The choice affects every other decision on this page. Both options work well when they match the house.

Storage tank (gas or electric)

  • Stores a reserve that can serve several fixtures at once
  • Simpler venting for electric, a flue or power vent for gas
  • Lower purchase price and widely stocked parts
  • Needs floor space and a drain pan
  • Wears out and needs replacing in time

Tankless or heat pump hybrid

  • Tankless heats on demand and frees floor space
  • Tankless often needs a bigger gas line and new venting
  • Heat pump models use less electricity but need air and a condensate drain
  • Both cost more to install and need setup that matches the house
  • Hard water means tankless units need periodic descaling

Our tankless water heater page covers venting, minimum flow and descaling in detail.

Fuel and venting decide the layout

An electric tank needs no vent, which gives you freedom on location, but it does need a 240 volt circuit sized to the unit's nameplate. We tell you what your panel needs before the job, since circuit work is scheduled separately from the plumbing.

A gas tank needs a flue to carry exhaust outside, combustion air, and a gas line sized for the burner. A natural draft flue rises through the roof and depends on heat, while a power vent unit uses a fan and can run through a side wall in plastic pipe. A direct vent unit also pulls its air from outside. Each has clearance rules, and the choice often comes down to where the wall or chimney is.

Every gas install includes a gas leak test on the new connection and a draft check at the flue. If you smell gas at any time, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 first.

The parts that go in with every new heater

These are the parts that protect the heater and your house. A cheap install skips them and an honest one includes them.

  • ✓
    Shutoff valve on the cold supplyA full-port valve on the inlet lets the heater be isolated for service without turning off the whole house.
  • ✓
    Temperature and pressure relief valveThe relief valve opens before pressure or heat get dangerous, and its discharge pipe runs to a safe spot and ends near the floor.
  • ✓
    Expansion tankOn a closed system, where a check valve or pressure reducing valve stops water from backing into the street, a small tank takes up expansion as water heats.
  • ✓
    Drain pan with a piped drainA pan under the heater catches small leaks and leads them to a drain, which matters most upstairs or in finished space.
  • ✓
    Flexible connectors and dielectric fittingsThese prevent corrosion between dissimilar metals and make later service easier.
  • ✓
    Venting and combustion air for gasSized and pitched correctly, with a draft check after it is lit.

Permits, licensing and Chesterfield's water

Worth knowing

New installs and relocations are more likely to trigger permit questions than a swap. The City of Chesterfield contracts with St. Louis County for permitting and inspection, so ask the City or St. Louis County before work that may need a permit. Neighbors in Town and Country should ask their own city office for the same answer.

Missouri leaves plumber licensing to cities and counties, so confirm any plumber's licence with the building department where the job is. Missouri American Water's 2021 report put hardness at 66 to 170 mg/L across its Meramec and Missouri River plants, so a new heater benefits from a yearly flush and, if scale is heavy, a water softener.

What shapes the cost of a new water heater installation

Installation prices swing more than replacement prices because the scope varies. These are the factors that move the number.

Type and size of the unitA 50 gallon tank, a power vent gas unit, a heat pump model and a tankless unit are different price classes before labor.
Moving versus staying putRelocating a heater means new supply lines, a new vent path and sometimes new gas or electrical runs.
Venting and combustion airStainless or plastic vent pipe, wall penetrations and roof flashing for gas units cost more than electric hookups.
Code and safety partsExpansion tank, relief valve discharge, pan and drain, plus shutoffs on the new location.
Permit and electrical workWhere permits apply, fees are added, and a new 240 volt circuit may be scheduled separately.

Insurance usually does not pay for planned upgrades or relocations. It may respond to sudden water damage if a poorly set up heater later fails, so keep the paperwork.

When installation is not the right job

If your current heater is only a few years old and just needs a part, repair is the cheaper path. If the existing tank has failed and the floor is wet, replacement is faster than a design project. If you are considering a full new set of lines to the house, plumbing installation and repiping cover that work.

We also will not install a heater where venting cannot be made legal and safe. If the plan does not work, we say so and offer an alternative location or fuel.
