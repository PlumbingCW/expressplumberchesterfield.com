Chesterfield Express Plumbing is a 24/7 plumbing dispatch line for Chesterfield, MO and four nearby towns. You call, a person answers, and a plumber is scheduled for the visit. This page explains what that means in practice, what we cover, what we leave alone, and how you can check any plumber before letting them into your home.

We would rather you know how we work before you call. Plumbing problems tend to arrive at bad times, and the decision to trust someone with your pipes should not be rushed.

What the 24/7 line is

The 24/7 line is a phone number that is answered at all hours, including nights, weekends and holidays. It is the only way to reach us. There is no web form, no email inbox to wait on and no online booking page. That keeps things simple when water is on the floor.

The person who answers listens to the problem, asks for the street address, and books the earliest visit the schedule allows. We offer same-day appointments when available. We do not promise an arrival time on the phone, because that would be a guess, and a guess is not useful when you are standing in a wet kitchen.

How a call goes from first ring to finished repair

1

You describe the problem

Say where it is, what you see and hear, and what you have shut off. Plain words are fine. A damp ceiling stain or a meter that keeps moving tells us a lot.

2

We ask a few questions

Expect questions about the address, the location of the main valve, and whether anyone is in danger. If you smell gas, we tell you to leave and call the gas utility or 911.

3

A plumber is scheduled

You get the earliest appointment available. If it cannot be today, we say so on the phone.

4

The price comes before the work

On site, the plumber confirms the cause, explains the repair and the cost, and waits for your go-ahead.

5

The job is finished and explained

You are told what was done, what was replaced and what to watch for afterward.

How to check any plumber's licence

Missouri does not have a single statewide plumber licence. State law works through cities over 15,000 people and first-class counties, so licensing is set locally. The practical step is to ask the building department for the city or county where the job is.

Ask whether the plumber or company is licensed there, and whether a permit is needed for your work. The City of Chesterfield contracts with St. Louis County for permitting and inspection, and the City's Department of Planning handles zoning approval. Permit rules vary, so ask before the work starts.

You can do this check on any plumber, including us. A reputable one will not mind the question.

How pricing works

We do not publish job prices because two drains that look alike can be very different problems. Instead, you hear a clear price before work starts. These are the factors that move it.

What is actually wrongA hair clog and a root-filled main line can both show up as a slow drain. Cost follows the cause.
AccessA pipe in an open basement ceiling is quicker to reach than one behind finished drywall or under a slab.
Parts and equipmentA standard fixture part costs less than a specialty valve or a water heater unit. Larger tools like a drain machine or camera add to the time.
Time of dayAfter-hours and weekend calls are part of the 24/7 line. We tell you the price for your call before work begins.
Scope you chooseSome jobs have a repair option and a replace option. We explain both and say honestly when a cheaper fix will do.

Many homeowners policies cover sudden accidental water damage but not wear or maintenance problems. Check your own policy, and take photos before cleanup.

Want the real number for your job? Call the 24/7 line, describe the work, and hear a clear price before anything starts.
