Drains and sewer lines

A slow sink or tub is usually a job for drain cleaning. When grease, scale or roots keep bringing the clog back, hydro jetting scours the pipe wall with high-pressure water instead of poking a hole through the blockage.

The main line leaving the house is a separate problem. Sewer line cleaning clears it, and sewer inspection with a pipe camera shows what is inside. When the pipe has cracked, bellied or collapsed, sewer line repair replaces or relines the damaged section.

Water heaters

A tank that gives lukewarm water, rumbles or leaks at a fitting often starts with water heater repair. If the tank itself is leaking or old, water heater replacement is the honest answer.

For a new location, a different fuel or a first-time setup, water heater installation covers venting, gas or electric connections and a drain pan. Households that want endless hot water can look at a tankless water heater, which needs correct venting and, in this area, attention to scale.

Leaks, burst pipes and old supply lines

A split pipe floods fast, so burst pipe repair is the one to call first, after you shut off the main valve. A quieter problem, such as a meter that keeps moving with everything off, needs leak detection to find the leak without opening every wall.

When pipes leak repeatedly or the water looks brown, repiping may cost less over time than chasing each failure. We explain both paths and say honestly when a spot repair will hold.

Toilets, faucets and disposals

A toilet that runs, rocks or flushes weakly is covered by toilet repair. A faucet that drips, sprays sideways or runs weak is a faucet repair job, and sometimes a replacement part is cheaper than a new fixture.

Under the sink, a unit that hums but does not spin or leaks from the bottom needs garbage disposal repair. Small fixes like these are rarely emergencies, and a plumber can often finish them in one visit with parts carried on the truck, though a leaking supply line should be shut off at the stop valve until we arrive.

Planned work, basements and water quality

New fixtures, appliance hookups and added lines fall under plumbing installation. In a low basement, sump pump installation and repair keeps groundwater out, while basement waterproofing deals with how water gets in.

Scale on fixtures and a chalky film on glassware point to hard water. Water softener and filtration work addresses that. Ask Missouri American Water for its latest report and which plant serves your address before you buy anything.

Businesses and larger buildings

Shops, offices and restaurants have long drain runs, many fixtures and little tolerance for downtime. Commercial plumbing covers those buildings, and the same 24/7 line applies. Tell us the suite number and who can approve work when you call.

Emergency work versus planned work

Some jobs cannot wait. Water that will not stop, sewage in a drain, a leaking tank and no hot water in freezing weather all justify an immediate call. Our emergency plumber in Chesterfield page covers the first minutes and when to call.

Planned work can be booked for a convenient day. Examples include new fixtures, a water heater upgrade while the old one still works, or a camera check before you buy a house. Booking early usually gives more choice of time. We serve Chesterfield and nearby towns such as Ballwin and Creve Coeur.

Whichever kind of job you have, the call works the same way. You describe the problem, we confirm the street address, and the plumber explains the cost on site before any work begins. If the cheaper fix is the right fix, we say that too.
