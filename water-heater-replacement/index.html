When a failed water heater is flooding your floor

A tank can lose its contents quickly once the lining splits, and 40 to 50 gallons across a finished basement carpet is a real loss. If water is spreading, close the cold supply to the heater, then the main if it keeps coming. Call the 24/7 line while you do it.

If the heater sits near the electrical panel or on a damp slab, stay out of standing water. Shut off the breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water. Do not touch a gas unit's control if you smell gas. Leave and call the gas utility or 911.

Sizing a replacement in plain terms

Bigger is not automatically better. An oversized tank wastes energy keeping extra water hot, and an undersized one runs out in the middle of a shower.

Count the hot water habits

People in the house, bathrooms, a soaking tub, a dishwasher and a laundry load at the same time all add up. A household of two and a household of six use very different amounts.

Read the first hour rating

The yellow label lists how much hot water a tank can deliver in one hour starting full. Compare that to your busiest morning, not an average day.

Match the footprint

A closet or alcove that held a 40 gallon unit may not hold a taller heat pump model, which also needs clearance and airflow.

Check the fuel connections you already have

A 240 volt circuit, a gas line and a working flue are all in place for a like-for-like swap. Changing fuel means building one of them.

Think about recovery

Gas tanks heat water faster than standard electric ones. A heat pump model recovers more slowly unless it is switched to its electric mode.

Is the tank past saving?

Rust water, a leaking base or a failed heating element all point to the same decision.

What happens during the removal and hookup visit

1

Shut down and drain

We close the supply, secure power or gas, attach a hose to the drain valve and empty the tank, moving water to a floor drain or outside.

2

Disconnect and remove

Water lines, gas connector or electrical whip, and any venting come off. The old tank is carried out and hauled away.

3

Set the new unit

A new drain pan with a piped drain goes under it where the layout allows. The tank is leveled and placed where service access is clear.

4

Connect and protect

New flex connectors, shutoff valves, a relief valve with a proper discharge pipe, and an expansion tank where the system is closed are installed. Gas units get a combustion draft check and a gas leak test.

5

Fill, purge, test

We fill the tank with a hot tap open to push out air, power it on, and confirm temperature, pressure and leak-free joints before we leave.

Switching from gas to a heat pump water heater

Some homeowners ask about replacing a gas tank with a heat pump model. It can be done, but it is a bigger job than a like-for-like swap. It needs a 240 volt circuit, a condensate drain, enough open air around the unit, and a spot where cooler exhaust air will not bother anyone.

Heat pump units are taller and recover more slowly in their efficient mode. They also cool the room they sit in, which is welcome in a hot garage and less so in a small finished space. We do not quote rebates or tax credits. Ask your utility or the manufacturer what applies, and we will give you the install side clearly.

Tank, tankless or heat pump: which swap fits

The same budget buys very different results depending on the house. Here is how the two most common paths compare for a failed tank.

Another storage tank

  • Fits the existing pipes, venting and electrical with few changes
  • Lowest installed complexity and the quickest same-day swap
  • Holds a reserve, so several fixtures can run at once
  • Standby heat loss, and the tank will need replacing again
  • Sizing is simple and replacement parts are widely stocked

Tankless conversion

  • Heats on demand, with no tank to flood a floor
  • Needs a bigger gas line or heavy electrical service and new venting
  • Frees wall or floor space
  • Needs flushing to deal with scale in hard water
  • Costs more up front, so confirm the house can support it first

Read the full picture on our tankless water heater page before deciding. A quick swap is also possible now and a conversion later, once you have time to plan.

Permits and licensing for a water heater swap in Chesterfield

Worth knowing

The City of Chesterfield contracts with St. Louis County for permitting and inspection, and the City's Department of Planning handles zoning approval. Permit rules vary, so ask the City or St. Louis County before work that may need a permit, and ask whichever office handles your address.

Missouri has no single statewide plumber licence. Plumber licensing in Missouri is set locally, so ask the building department where the job is, and confirm any plumber's licence with it. Neighbors in Wildwood are covered by their own city and county offices, not Chesterfield's.

Hard water and how long your next tank will last

Missouri American Water's 2021 St. Louis region report listed Missouri River plant hardness from 76 to 170 mg/L, about 4.4 to 9.9 grains per gallon. Moderately hard water leaves scale that shortens heater life if it is never flushed. Ask Missouri American Water for the latest report and which plant serves you.

A yearly flush, an anode rod check and a water softener or filter if scale is heavy all help your next tank last. A heater that reaches the end of a normal life and fails is not a sign of neglect. One that fails young usually is.
