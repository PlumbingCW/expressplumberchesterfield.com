Under Sink Garbage Disposal Installation in Chesterfield, MO

Swapping or adding a disposal is a manageable weekend job when you know the order of steps and where the traps are.

Installing an under sink garbage disposal in Chesterfield, MO comes down to five stages: kill the power, set the new mounting ring in the sink flange, hang the disposal, connect the dishwasher and drain, then power up and test for leaks. Replacing an existing unit with the same brand is the easiest case because the mounting hardware often reuses.

A first-time install, a switch to a different brand, or a new electrical connection takes longer and has more ways to go wrong. This guide lists the tools, the order of work, the cord versus hardwire decision and the points where calling a plumber is the smarter move.

Tools and parts to lay out before you start

Having everything within reach saves trips and keeps the sink out of service for less time.

  • ✓
    The new disposal, its mounting assembly and any included flange gasket
  • ✓
    Plumber's putty, a flathead and Phillips screwdriver, and a hammer for the dishwasher knockout
  • ✓
    Channel-lock pliers, a hacksaw or tubing cutter, and a bucket
  • ✓
    Wire nuts and a voltage tester if you are wiring the unit
  • ✓
    A new dishwasher drain hose clamp and a power cord kit if your model needs one
  • ✓
    A flashlight and a few rags for the cabinet floor

The installation sequence from old unit to first run

1

Cut the power and clear the cabinet

Turn off the breaker for the disposal and confirm with a voltage tester. Empty the cabinet and place a bucket under the trap.

2

Remove the old unit

Disconnect the drain tube and dishwasher hose. Loosen the mounting ring by rotating its lugs, then lift the old disposal out. Scrape away old putty from the sink flange.

3

Seat the new flange and mounting ring

Press a rope of plumber's putty under the new sink flange, set it in the drain opening and tighten the mounting ring from below. Wipe the squeezed-out putty.

4

Knock out the dishwasher plug

If a dishwasher drains into the unit, tap the plastic plug out of the inlet with a hammer and screwdriver. Fish the loose plug out of the chamber so it cannot jam the grinder.

5

Hang the disposal and align the trap

Lift the unit onto the mounting ring and rotate it until the discharge points toward the drain. Connect the discharge tube and trim it, so the trap runs slightly downhill to the wall.

6

Wire it, power up and test

Connect the wires, close the cover plate, restore power and run water. Check every joint with dry paper towel, then run the unit for a few seconds.

Cord or hardwire: choosing the electrical connection

The way a disposal gets power affects both convenience and safety.

Plug-in cord

  • Easier to replace the unit later without touching wiring
  • Needs a grounded outlet under the sink, usually switched at the wall
  • Not every kit includes a cord, so check the box before you buy
  • Local rules may affect how and where the outlet is installed

Direct hardwire

  • No visible cord, which keeps the cabinet tidier
  • Connects through a junction box and wire nuts, which demands a careful, safe hand
  • Better when no outlet exists under the sink
  • If you are unsure about wiring, hire a licensed electrician or plumber

Trap alignment and the leak test most people rush

The discharge tube has to line up with the trap without strain. If you force it, a joint will weep within days. Dry-fit the pieces first, mark the tube, cut it square and let the slope carry water toward the wall.

For the leak test, plug the sink, fill it, then pull the plug while the unit is still off so water flows through the whole path. Run your hand around each joint. Then run the disposal with cold water for 15 seconds. Check again after an hour, because a slow drip only shows up once things settle.

When to hand the job to a plumber

Call if the cabinet floor is already soft, if the sink flange is cracked, or if the old drain pipe is corroded enough to crumble when you touch it. Call too if you have no outlet and no wiring experience, or if the disposal keeps backing up into the second basin.
