Sewer line cleaning, drain cleaning, jetting and repair: which one do you need?

These four services get mixed up on the phone, and the wrong guess costs a trip. Fixture clogs belong to drain cleaning, washed-on grease belongs to hydro jetting, and broken pipe belongs to sewer line repair.

What usually blocks a main sewer line

The cause shapes the machine we bring, so we ask a few questions before we roll the truck.

Tree and shrub roots

Fine roots enter at joints and grow into a mat that catches paper. They are the most common blockage in older clay and cast iron laterals.

Grease and soap scale

Fat builds along the pipe floor and narrows the bore until paper stops moving. It softens with hot water and hardens again as it cools.

Wipes and hygiene products

Flushable wipes do not break down. They wrap on rough joints and form a rope that catches everything after it.

A belly or offset joint

A dip in the pipe holds water and solids, and a shifted joint creates a ledge. Cleaning clears it, but it fills again.

A collapsed section

If the cable will not pass a certain point, the pipe may have failed. That is a repair, not a cleaning, issue.

Main line clogged?

If every drain in the house is slow, the blockage is probably past the last branch.

How we clear a blocked main from the cleanout

1

Find the cleanout

A capped pipe in the basement, crawlspace or yard gives direct access to the main. If none exists, we can work through a removed toilet.

2

Choose cutter or jetter

A drum machine with a root cutter suits a root mass. A water jetter suits grease and silt. Either is chosen after we hear the symptoms.

3

Open the line

The head is fed in slowly and the operator watches the cable tension. A sudden give usually means the blockage has been cut through.

4

Flush and test

We run water and confirm the line drains without gurgling. We watch the cleanout for backflow from further down the line.

5

Say what we found

Roots, grease, paper and wipes each leave a different trace on the head. We describe it and recommend a camera if a repeat clog seems likely.

A clogged main, then what

Clearing a main is the first half of the job. The second half is deciding why it clogged. A one-time backup after a heavy wipe habit or a grease dump may never repeat. A backup that returns every few months is a pipe problem.

A sewer camera inspection after the line is open shows root entry points, offset joints and bellies. We do not make that camera run mandatory, but if you plan to buy, sell or keep the house, the footage is useful. The same crew serves homes in Creve Coeur and other nearby towns.

Whose pipe is it, and where does the City come in?

Worth knowing

The City of Chesterfield says the property owner is responsible for the lateral sewer service line. Cleaning that line is the owner's cost, and routine cabling is not reimbursable. For anything past your property line, ask the City's Department of Public Works who owns the pipe at your address.

If cabling by a licensed plumbing company fails to clear the lateral, the City's Residential Sanitary Sewer Lateral Repair Program may cover certain repairs up to $15,000, with a non-refundable $200 application. Applications use the cabling invoice, the prior year's tax receipt showing the sewer lateral fee and a notarized hold-harmless agreement. Keep that invoice.
